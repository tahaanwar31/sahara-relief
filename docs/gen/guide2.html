Why we built it

The problem, in numbers

The technology, and why

Every choice was made for one of four reasons: it works offline, it is explainable, it is private, it is free to run

Web app

React + TypeScript installable app, Urdu and English, works offline, recorded voice

→

Python service

FastAPI. Rules, AI checks, decisions, explanations, the API

→

Database

Postgres on Supabase. Row locks, unchangeable diary, rules, consent

The service also talks to three mock government services: identity (like NADRA), household survey (like NSER) and payments (like Raast).
The one rule of the designThe web app talks only to our service, never straight to the database. The service then talks to the database as the signed-in person, so the database itself refuses to show anyone else's rows. A bug in our code cannot leak someone's data.

The front end: what people see

ToolWhy we chose it
React 18 + TypeScriptLarge community and reusable screens. TypeScript catches many mistakes before the app runs.
Vite + Tailwind CSSFast builds and small files. Tailwind gives a consistent design and supports right-to-left Urdu.
Installable web app (PWA) + service workerOpens from the browser like an app, with no app store, and keeps working with no signal. A keypad-to-smartphone upgrade is cheaper than a new app.
IndexedDB (idb)Stores the offline outbox and a copy of her claim on the phone, so a dead battery loses nothing.
Recorded voice (edge-tts)154 Urdu and English prompts recorded once and stored in the app, so they play with no internet. Live text-to-speech would need signal.

The back end: the brain

ToolWhy we chose it
Python + FastAPI + UvicornPython is the language of machine learning, so the AI and the API live together. FastAPI checks every input and writes the Swagger documentation for free.
psycopg 3A direct database connection, so each request can run as the signed-in person and the database applies that person's row locks.
scikit-learn, numpySmall, explainable models that train in seconds on a laptop CPU.
RapidOCR + onnxruntimeReads the ID card on the CPU with no internet and no graphics card.
OpenCV (YuNet, SFace)Finds and compares the face on the card with the face in the government record.
rapidfuzzFast name comparison for spelling variants (Muhammad, Mohammad, Muhammed).

The database

ChoiceWhy
Postgres (SQL)Money and identity need exact rules between tables. SQL gives strict constraints and row-level security. A document store such as MongoDB or Firebase cannot enforce "this person sees only her own rows" as strongly.
Supabase (hosted Postgres)Managed Postgres with login and storage, close to Pakistan (Mumbai). A local database is the fallback for the demo.
Row locks (row-level security)The database, not the screen, decides who may see which row. A citizen sees her claim; a helper sees only the family he has open; an admin sees only cases of his office with active consent.
Hash-chained diaryEvery important action is written once and linked to the one before. Nobody can edit or delete it, and a "Verify the diary" button recomputes the chain.
6 migrations, seed dataThe database can be rebuilt from scratch, in order, for any teammate or judge.

Other decisions, and the reason for each

Our own small models, not a chat AI

No real data may leave the system. A small calibrated model gives a trustworthy confidence ("I am not sure" needs one), explains itself, and costs nothing to run. A chat model sounds sure even when wrong.

Hard rules before any AI score

No consent, an expired ID or an edited paper always blocks automatic approval. Rules are data in a safe language, not code, so an admin can change them with versions.

Docker + Render for hosting (planned)

One image holds the service and the built web app. We did not choose Vercel: it suits websites, while our service is a long-running Python program that loads AI models.

Mock government services behind adapters

Each mock has the shape a real one would. Replacing it later means changing one adapter, not the system.

Testing: pytest (179 service tests), Node test runner (50 database tests), Vitest (27 web tests) and Playwright with real Chrome (23 click-through checks across all three roles).

The three roles

One login, one role. Each role sees different screens, and the database enforces it
👩

Citizen

Shown as "Person" in the app. The woman who claims the payment

What she does

1 Start2 Permission3 Her ID number4 Household size5 ID card photos6 Account number7 Send8 Answer
  • Before steps 2 to 7, a short practice demo plays: a hand taps a mock pad with made-up details and a voice explains, in Urdu or English. She then does it herself. Nothing in the demo is sent.
  • She types her ID number; the system shows her own record and asks "Is this you?". Typing someone else's number shows only that name, and her papers are then compared with that person's record.
  • She is asked how many people live in her home. The system answers only yes or no and never shows the number.
  • Only two photos: the ID card front and back. The system also checks that each photo is the right side.
  • She types her own mobile account number. Nothing is ever read back to her.
  • Three results: Approved (paid at once, in the mock), Please send one more thing, or A person will look with the exact reason.
  • After sending, "My claim" shows one box that updates by itself: Our people are checking your claim, the staff's request with a Send it now button, or your payment has been sent, our staff will call you to confirm.

Her protections

  • Her permission is recorded with a receipt. She can take it back at any time in "My data", and staff access stops at once.
  • She sees who has looked at her data.
  • Progress is saved after every step and on the phone; a 6-digit resume code continues on another phone.
  • Voice on every page, big buttons, a number pad, no reading needed.
  • With no signal, photos and the final send wait on the phone and go out by themselves.
  • "Is it real?" checks any SMS for scams and only warns, never blocks.
🤝

Helper

A community worker who helps a woman claim, from his own phone

What he does

  • Sees only the families assigned to him. Names show as F*** B*** until she gives permission.
  • To open a family he asks her three things and types the answers: her ID number, her date of birth and her household size. The server checks them against the government record and answers only yes or no, so he never sees the record. Three wrong tries lock the family for 30 minutes.
  • Right answers record her permission (to the office and to him), with a diary line. He then does the claim with her; her ID number and household size are not asked twice.
  • One family at a time, so papers cannot mix.

What he cannot do

  • Approve or reject anything.
  • See a family he is not assigned to, or a name before permission.
  • Give permission for her without her three details.
  • Send the money to himself: a payout account that matches a helper's own number is held for a person.
  • Change rules or read the diary.
🛡

Admin

Programme staff: reviews cases, sets rules, checks the record

What he does

  • Review queue: only claims the system could not decide, least sure first, only for his own office and only with active consent. In the demo it starts empty.
  • Case page: the decision with its checks and reasons; the "I am not sure" notes; a side-by-side table of her registration record, the government record and what her ID card photo says (differences in red, plus a face row); her papers, which he can open (every opening is recorded).
  • Decide: Approve, Reject, Hold, or Ask for more proof, choosing which paper (ID front, back, both or another) and writing a message. Payments over Rs 20,000 need a second admin.
  • Rules: versioned rule sets, never edited in place; a Digital Twin replays past cases under a draft before it goes live; a cost slider shows where the approval line is cheapest.
  • Evaluation and diary: accuracy, false alarms, speed; "Verify the diary" checks nobody changed history.

What the database stops

  • Approving his own case or one he helped with.
  • Deciding a case after consent was taken back.
  • Approving with an open hard flag (for example the same card photo sent by two different people).
  • Editing or deleting a decision or the diary.
  • Reading the identity table directly: he sees cases, decisions and the diary, with the ID number masked.

The AI features

What each one does, why it exists, how it works, and how well it did
How the AI is used safely Hard rules run first and no score can override them. The checks and the model then decide between three outcomes: approve alone (only above 97% sure and no flag), ask for one more paper, or send to a person. Auto-reject is off, so the machine never says "no" alone. All numbers below come from synthetic data, so they show the method works, not how it would perform on real people.

1. ID card reading and side check

Computer visionPretrained, not trained by us

What it does

Reads the name, father's name, ID number and birth date off the card photo, tells the front from the back, and compares with the government record.

Why

A clerk does this by eye today. It catches a wrong card and a wrong side at once and gives the exact reason.

How it works

RapidOCR (small open-source models on the CPU) reads the text on a fixed card layout. The back is recognised by its address lines. Numbers match digit by digit; names match ignoring case, with small spelling slips allowed.

Result: caught every planted wrong name and number in our tests. Limit: a clean template card, not a real dirty photo.

2. Face match

Computer visionPretrained; threshold set by us

What it does

Compares the face on the card with the photo in the government record.

Why

Stops a stranger using someone else's card.

How it works

OpenCV YuNet finds the face; SFace turns it into 128 numbers; two faces are "the same" at cosine similarity 0.50 or more. We chose the stricter line: a wrong "no match" only sends the claim to a person, a wrong "match" would wave a stranger through.

Result: caught every wrong face in our tests. Limit: the line was set on a handful of AI-generated faces and needs a fairness test on real people.

3. Name and duplicate matching

Classic algorithm

What it does

Says whether two records are the same person: SAME, MAYBE or DIFFERENT.

Why

Pakistani names are spelled many ways. Merging the wrong two people, or missing a duplicate, both cost money.

How it works

Cleans the names (Muhammad, Mohammad, Muhammed become one; titles such as Bibi are dropped), then scores name 30%, ID number 30%, father's name 20%, date of birth 15%, district 5%. Never SAME on a name alone; unsure goes to a person.

Result: 0 false merges on 10 labelled pairs. Limit: a small test.

4. The decision model

Machine learningTrained by us

What it does

Gives one honest number, "how likely is this claim truly eligible?", that the engine uses to choose approve, ask or person.

Why

"I am not sure" needs a confidence that can be trusted, so the number is calibrated: when it says 97%, about 97 in 100 should be right.

How it works

A logistic regression on 21 numbers (name match, document confidence, household score against the cutoff, flags, payout risk and more), wrapped in isotonic calibration. Trained on 12,000 synthetic cases from our own generator, with a fixed seed so it repeats.

Result (2,000 fresh cases): 38% approved alone, 56% to a person, 6% asked for proof; 14 wrong approvals of 758; 0 wrong rejections; about 6.6 ms a decision. Limit: trained and tested on data from the same generator.

5. Scam message checker

Machine learningTrained by us

What it does

Reads an SMS and says Looks real, Be careful, or Looks like a scam, with plain reasons in Urdu and English.

Why

Fake relief messages are common and cost families a quarter's money. It only warns; it never blocks.

How it works

Letter pieces of the text (works for English, Roman Urdu and Urdu script) plus nine rule signals such as asking for a PIN, a fee, an urgent threat or a strange link, fed to a logistic regression. A direct ask for a PIN, OTP or ID number is never labelled safe.

Result: 92.9% on 56 test messages; 1 false alarm in 27 safe messages; 1 missed scam. Limit: about 480 training messages that we wrote ourselves.

6. Photo integrity checks

Image maths

What it does

Checks every photo for blur, signs of editing, and whether the same picture was sent before, including by another person.

Why

Catches blurry photos (ask again), pasted or edited papers, and one card used for two families.

How it works

A sharpness score; error-level analysis (a pasted area compresses differently); file and picture fingerprints. An unclear photo gets "please take it again"; the system never guesses.

Result: 21 of 21 invented images classed as expected. Limit: a skilled forgery would pass; a person always decides doubtful cases.

7. "I am not sure" notes and similar cases

Rules, not learned

What it does

Gives the admin one page: what was checked, why it is unsure, what is missing, what to ask, plus the three past labelled cases with the closest household score.

Why

A person who must decide should not have to dig. This is the "human hand-off" done well.

How it works

Built from the decision trace and facts by rules and templates. It is useful but it is not a learned model, and we say so.

Result: created for every claim sent to a person.

8. Digital Twin and cost curve

Simulation

What it does

Shows what a rule change would do before it goes live, and where the approval line is cheapest.

Why

Policy teams change rules often. A wrong change affects thousands of families.

How it works

Replays up to 500 past cases under a draft rule and counts who changes zone and the total cost (wrong approval 100, wrong rejection 20, person reviewing 3, asking for proof 1). The cost curve plots expected cost against the approval line.

Example from the demo: a draft version moved 56 cases (22 from approved to person).

9. Policy answers with sources, and recorded voice

SearchPretrained voices

What they do

Policy search answers a plain question with the matching policy clause as its source and never invents an answer. The voice speaks every prompt, reason and practice demo.

Why

A woman, or an admin, can ask "why?" and get the real text. Voice means no reading is needed.

How they work

Search: word and word-pair matching over short clauses. Voice: Microsoft neural voices recorded once (154 files), played from the phone with no internet.

Limit: no voice input yet (speech to text was cut), and a native Urdu speaker must review the Urdu.

What we did not use: no hosted AI service and no large language model. There is an optional wrapper that could reword explanations, switched off by default and never used to make a decision.

5

Limitations

What we have not solved, in the order that matters most. Say these out loud before anyone asks

1. The helper commission problem

Biggest open risk

Studies and news reports say some agents and helpers take a cut (Rs 1,000 to 2,000) from the woman's payment, and she feels she cannot refuse. A helper beside her is exactly the person who can ask. Software alone cannot stop a person asking for cash after the money has reached her account.

What we did: the money goes to her own account, typed by her. A payout account that matches any helper's own number is held for a person. A helper only opens a family with three details she gives him, can hold one family at a time, and cannot approve anything. Every action is in an unchangeable diary.

What we did not do: we cannot see who owns an account (we only check its shape), and the three details are not secret (neighbours may know them). A planned safeguard, not built: after every payout, an automatic phone call asks each woman "press 1 if you received the full amount, press 2 if anyone took or asked for money", and a confirmed complaint removes the helper.

2. All data and numbers are synthetic

High

People, ID numbers, households, cases, scam messages and training data are invented, and we wrote the data generator. The accuracy numbers measure how well the models learned our own rules, not how they would do on real families.

Next: train on real, approved, de-identified data under a data agreement, and measure everything again.

3. The ID card reader and face match are tested on clean sample cards

High

The reader works on one fixed English card layout. A real card (Urdu text, glare, bent corners, a thumb over the corner) needs a stronger model. The face line (0.50) was set on a handful of AI-generated faces; it needs testing on different ages and skin tones for fairness. Only the demo person has a registry photo; for others the face check is skipped, never guessed.

Next: test on messy real photos, report the honest number, and run a fairness check.

4. Small test sets

Medium

Scam checker: 56 messages. Name matching: 10 pairs. Photo checks: 21 images. They show the method works but are too small to promise real accuracy. Edit detection is a simple heuristic that a skilled forger would pass.

5. The three services are mocks, and nothing is really paid

Medium

Identity (like NADRA), household survey (like NSER) and payment (like Raast) are made up, and the mock pays instantly after approval. A real payment rail confirms later and can fail, so a real system needs pending, failed and retry states.

6. Language and voice

Medium

There is no voice input: she listens and taps. The Urdu text and the computer voices have not been reviewed by a native speaker, and the longer new staff sentences appear in English on the Urdu staff view. Only Urdu and English exist; Sindhi, Pashto and Punjabi do not.

7. Weak internet was tested by simulation

Medium

We cut the connection in Chrome and the photo and the send waited and went through afterwards. We did not test on a real phone on a real network. The badge says only online or offline; there is no "weak signal" state.

8. Not yet deployed; some paths untested

Medium

The app is not on a public link yet. The Docker image is written but untested. The hosted-login mode (Supabase Auth) is written but untested; the login we run is the local one. Rate limiting lives in the service's memory, which is fine for one server only.

9. The record itself

Low

The diary cannot be changed by users or admins, but someone with full database-owner power could rewrite it. Helper proof by ID number, birth date and household size is not a secret; a one-time SMS code to her registered phone would be stronger. The scam checker only warns, by design.

6

How it scales

From one laptop to a programme with about 10 to 12 million households a quarter

The design already separates what must grow (photo checks, database, queues) from what stays small (the decision, about 6.6 ms). Scaling is mostly adding capacity and replacing mocks, not rewriting.

Phase 1: Pilot

one district, thousands of women
  • Real identity, survey and payment connections replace the three mocks (one adapter each)
  • Test the card reader on real photos; fairness check on the face model
  • Native Urdu review; add Sindhi and Pashto
  • Deploy on Render with Supabase; real phones on real networks
  • Build and test the payout confirmation call

Phase 2: Province

hundreds of thousands
  • Several service copies behind a load balancer (the service keeps no state)
  • Move rate limits to a shared store such as Redis
  • Photo analysis in background workers; photos in object storage, not local disk
  • Database connection pooler and read replicas; split big tables by quarter
  • Voice input in Urdu; USSD or SMS for keypad phones

Phase 3: National

millions of households
  • Auto-scaling workers for the quarter-start peak
  • Content network for the app and voice files
  • Publish the diary hash outside the database
  • Monitoring of accuracy, false alarms and fairness; retrain from approved human decisions
  • Same rails reused for other benefit programmes

What stays the same at any size

The rule that a person decides every "no".

The machine approves alone only above 97% sure, and never rejects alone.

Access enforced in the database.

More copies of the service do not weaken who can see which row.

An unchangeable record of every action.

The diary and decision history make the system auditable as it grows.

Honest numbers.

Every accuracy figure is labelled with the data it came from, and re-measured on real data before any claim of real performance.

Sahara Relief is a prototype that shows the last mile can be built so that the woman can use it, the helper cannot quietly misuse it, and the machine knows when to ask a person.
